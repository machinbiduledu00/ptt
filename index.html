How the calculation works

The calculator estimates the financial cost of waiting longer to get paid by applying your annual cost of capital to the number of additional days your cash is tied up.

Cost per invoice Invoice amount × Annual cost of capital × (Days difference ÷ 365)

For example, a €10,000 invoice moving from Net 60 to Net 30 with an annual cost of capital of 8% represents approximately €65.75 in financing value per invoice.

If you enter an early-payment discount, the calculator compares the cost of that discount with the estimated financing benefit of being paid earlier.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Net 30 and Net 60?

Net 30 means an invoice is due 30 days after the invoice date, while Net 60 gives the customer 60 days to pay. For the seller, Net 60 therefore means waiting an additional 30 days to receive the same amount of cash.

How much does Net 60 cost compared with Net 30?

The cost depends on the invoice amount and your cost of capital. For example, on a €10,000 invoice with an 8% annual cost of capital, waiting an additional 30 days represents approximately €65.75 in financing cost.

How do you calculate the cost of payment terms?

A simple estimate is calculated by multiplying the invoice amount by the annual cost of capital and by the difference in payment days divided by 365.

Is an early-payment discount worth it?

It depends on the size of the discount and the value of receiving the money earlier. If the discount costs more than the estimated financing benefit, it may not be financially worthwhile based on financing cost alone.

Is a 2% early-payment discount worth it?

A 2% early-payment discount means giving up 2% of the invoice value in exchange for earlier payment. On a €10,000 invoice, that discount costs €200. You can compare that cost with the financing benefit using the calculator above.

This calculator provides an estimate for informational purposes only. Actual financing costs may vary depending on your business, borrowing costs, cash-flow needs, taxes, fees, and payment behavior.
